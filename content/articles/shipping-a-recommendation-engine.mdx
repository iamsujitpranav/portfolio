At Emami Frankross the goal was concrete: better product recommendations on a
Rails e-commerce platform serving thousands of daily transactions — without
pausing feature work to rewrite the monolith.

## The shape of the solution

Rather than force ML into Rails, I gave it its own home:

1. **A FastAPI service** exposing recommendation endpoints.
2. **PyTorch embedding models** trained on browsing, cart, purchase, and
   engagement data.
3. **A thin client in Rails** that called the service and cached results in Redis.

The monolith didn't need to know how embeddings worked — only how to ask a
question and render an answer.

## Why this beat a rewrite

- **Independent deploys.** The model could iterate on its own cadence.
- **Blast radius.** A bad model version degraded to a sane fallback, not a
  checkout outage.
- **Reuse.** The same embeddings later powered search relevance work alongside
  Algolia.

Release velocity doubled over the same period, and search relevance measurably
improved — proof that you can bolt intelligence onto a mature system if you
respect its boundaries.
