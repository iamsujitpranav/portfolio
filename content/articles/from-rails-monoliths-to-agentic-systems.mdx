For most of my career the hard problems were about **scale and structure**: turning
a Rails monolith into resilient microservices, keeping a high-traffic e-commerce
platform up during a sale, shaving 40% off incident-response time with the right
observability stack.

That work still matters. But over the last three years the frontier moved. The
hardest, most interesting layer is now making software that **reasons** —
recommendation engines, semantic search, and agentic developer workflows that
actually earn their place in production.

## What carried over

The instincts from a decade of backend work transfer directly:

- **Boundaries.** An LLM integration is a service boundary like any other — it
  needs timeouts, retries, graceful degradation, and a clear contract.
- **Observability.** You cannot ship a RAG pipeline you can't see. The same
  Grafana + New Relic discipline applies to token spend and latency.
- **Cost as a first-class constraint.** Prompt caching and model selection are
  the new query optimization.

## What was new

Structured outputs, tool use, and the Model Context Protocol turned "call the
model" into "compose an agent." At Arccaa I leaned on Claude MCP and Codex MCP for
repository analysis, code review, and documentation — the same way I'd once leaned
on Sidekiq for background work.

The lesson: treat AI as an **engineering discipline**, not a demo. Ship it with
tests, review, and rollback, and it becomes just another dependable part of the
system.
